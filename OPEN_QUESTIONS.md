# Open questions

Pick any. Say so in an issue so we do not duplicate work.

1. **Is this known?** Prior art on how long a finite Rule 30 seed can follow a prescribed center column past its radius. Related: Jen (1986/1990), Kopra, Condrey (arXiv:2609.09431), Rowland ("Local nested structure in rule 30", 2006). A pointer, even "this is trivially true because...", is valuable.
2. **Independent replication.** Reimplement the gap computation from the README description (no peeking at `gapscan.c`) and compare with `results/gap_period2_exact.csv`. Disagreements are the most useful thing you can send.
3. **Explain the small gap.** Is `gap(w) <= C log2 w` provable for some `C`? The data need `C >= 4` (gap 17 at `w = 19`) and do not distinguish a log law from other slow growth. Why the horizon jumps from 26 to 36 at `w = 19` and then holds for ten widths is unexplained too; the seeds that reach it may be the place to start. The cells just past the seed's edge take only a few dozen distinct patterns over all right halves; that structure is the likeliest handle. Rowland's period-doubling of Rule 30's edge diagonals may be relevant.
4. **Prove it for one period above 1.** Even period 2 with a finite (non-uniform) gap for every width would be new (period 1 is a theorem here). One failed attempt, with the mechanism it found and where it stopped, is in [PERIOD2_NOTES.md](PERIOD2_NOTES.md); its section 7 lists smaller questions that came out of it. What it did prove (section 6a, `lean/Repeat.lean`): the two columns beside the center cannot repeat a stretch longer than the pattern's left width. Closing the case from there needs a bound on zero runs inside the pattern, or some other control of repetitions late in the run.
5. **Other rules (partly answered).** I compared the 8 left-permutive rules with `f = l XOR g(c,r)` and `g(0,0)=0` (Wolfram codes 30, 60, 90, 120, 150, 180, 210, 240) at width 12, for eight target patterns. A small gap for a *non-constant* repeating target is generic: all eight rules, even the trivial pure shift (240), give gaps of only 1 to 10. What is special about Rule 30 is that even the *constant* targets (all 0, all 1) have a finite gap (Condrey's theorem); in the other rules some finite seed follows the all-zero pattern forever. So the slow growth is less specific to Rule 30 than it may look. Later comparison at widths 14 to 26 (rules 30, 90, 120, 180, 210; targets `01`, `10`, `001`, `011`, `00101`): Rule 30's gaps are the largest (up to 16) and the most irregular; rule 120 stays around 5; rules 90, 180 and 210 stay at 1 to 3. At these widths Rule 30's growth cannot be told apart from the others'. Open: do any of the other rules' gaps grow at larger widths, and is Rule 30's growth specific to it? Is there a general theorem for left-permutive rules? Rules with `g(0,0)=1` (45, 135, ...) have no quiescent zero background and are not covered.
6. **Larger scale.** Exhaustive enumeration to width 38 (period 2) and exact SAT to 82 (`src/mint.py`; width 82 took 3.6 hours). Extending either, adding UNSAT certificates (DRAT/LRAT) to the SAT runs, or checking larger periods, would show whether slow growth persists.
7. **The backgrounds.** For alternating traces, the forced left half sometimes has a periodic tail, always one of a few Rule 30 backgrounds (ring sizes 7, 155, 728, with an 84-ring one that never occurs as a tail). Why exactly these? The block sizes found so far (7, 14 or 28, 31, 104 = 8 x 13) are each a power of 2 times `1 + r + r^2`, and so are the ring sizes 84 = 4 x 21 and 728 = 8 x 91 (155 = 5 x 31 is not). Caveats: this family was chosen after seeing the data, 39 of the integers up to 300 have that form, and the 34 other members up to 300 gave no background in the searches. So it looks necessary rather than generating, if it means anything. Is it a coincidence?
8. **Lean.** State and prove any of the above, or give a cleaner formalization of the reduction. Already there: the period-1 theorem (`ConstColumn.lean`), the reduction to Problem 1 (`Reduction.lean`), a forced cell under an alternating column (`Period2.lean`), and the repetition bound with the two-adjacent-columns theorem for columns 0 and -1 (`Repeat.lean`).
